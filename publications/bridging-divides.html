Journal Article

Bridging Divides or Widening Gaps? Nonprofit Organizations’ Efforts for Migrant Inclusion in Two Global Cities

Yan Long, Wei Luo, Berta Terzieva
Nonprofit and Voluntary Sector Quarterly, 55(4), 1049–1074, 2026 (first published online 2025).
Publisher Link (DOI)

Abstract

Nonprofits are often expected to foster migrant inclusion, yet their internal practices can also reproduce local inequalities. Comparing organizations in Vienna and Shenzhen, this study examines how state and market relationships, representative governance, and staff–beneficiary contact shape how closely organizations’ work reflects the migrant composition of their communities.

Research Summary

Research Question

What helps nonprofit organizations include, reach, and serve migrants in proportion to their presence in local communities?

Main Finding

Representative staffing improves diversity alignment when it is supported by sustained interpersonal contact. Formal representative procedures alone do not guarantee inclusion and can sometimes widen organizational gaps.

Data and Methods

Comparative analysis of organizational survey data from nonprofit organizations in Vienna and Shenzhen.
