Journal Article

Neighborhood Effects on Integrative Organizational Practices in Five Global Cities

Christof Brandtner, Krystal Laryea, Gowun Park, Wei Luo, Michael Meyer, David Suárez, Hokyu Hwang, Walter W. Powell
Nature Cities, 1(12), 853–860, 2024.
Publisher Link (DOI)

Abstract

Civil society organizations connect people across social differences, but their integrative practices are unevenly distributed across urban neighborhoods. Linking organizational surveys with neighborhood data from five global cities, this study examines how local affluence and migrant diversity shape social and systemic integration.

Research Summary

Research Question

How do neighborhood income and migrant diversity shape the ways civil society organizations connect people and organizations?

Main Findings

Practices that connect people to one another are more common in poorer neighborhoods. Practices that connect people and organizations to broader organizational systems are more common in heterogeneous neighborhoods, especially affluent ones.

Data and Methods

Survey data on 863 civil society organizations in 536 neighborhoods across San Francisco, Seattle, Shenzhen, Sydney, and Vienna, combined with neighborhood-level data.
