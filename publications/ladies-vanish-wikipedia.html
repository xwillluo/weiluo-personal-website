Journal Article

The Ladies Vanish? American Sociology and the Genealogy of its Missing Women on Wikipedia

Wei Luo, Julia Adams, Hannah Brueckner
Comparative Sociology, 17(5), 519–556, 2018.
Publisher Link (DOI)

Abstract

Many notable women sociologists are absent from the canonical history of American sociology. This article asks whether Wikipedia’s crowd-sourced model corrects that exclusion or reproduces it. By tracing the interaction between disciplinary histories and digital knowledge production, the authors identify four patterns through which women are represented: remembered, neglected, erased, and recovered. Wikipedia can create new opportunities for recognition, but its reliance on established sources and notability standards often carries omissions and distortions from print canons into the digital record. The result is a hybrid knowledge space in which gendered disciplinary memory is reproduced but can also be contested.

Summary

Research Question

Why do notable women sociologists disappear from disciplinary history, and does Wikipedia recover them or reproduce their exclusion?

Main Argument and Findings

Women’s representation is shaped by the interaction between expert-made disciplinary canons and crowd-sourced digital knowledge. Wikipedia’s dependence on existing published sources often reproduces established exclusions. Women may be remembered, neglected, erased, or recovered depending on professional gatekeeping, marriage and collaboration, institutional location, and later efforts of historical reconstruction.

Data and Methods

A genealogical comparison of women sociologists’ representation in canonical disciplinary histories, biographical sourcebooks, and Wikipedia, with detailed attention to early American sociology and the Chicago School.

Contribution

The article provides a framework for studying disciplinary memory across print and digital media. It shows that open, crowd-sourced platforms do not automatically correct gender bias because their standards and sources are embedded in older systems of recognition.
