Journal Article

Taming Violence: The Shanghai Green Gang and its Self-Legitimation Claims in the Early Twentieth Century

Wei Luo
Social Science History, 47(4), 585–608, 2023 (first published online 2022).
Publisher Link (DOI)

Abstract

Historical accounts often portray the Shanghai Green Gang either as a modern criminal syndicate serving capitalist interests or as a continuation of traditional Chinese secret societies. This article connects those perspectives by examining the gang’s own work of self-legitimation. Green Gang actors reframed violence as disciplinary, revolutionary, and nationalistic rather than merely criminal. Together with the organization’s involvement in state building and resource extraction, this cultural work helped establish its prominence and encouraged multiple political authorities to recognize and promote it. The article shows how an extralegal organization converted coercive capacity into claims of public legitimacy.

Summary

Research Question

How did the Shanghai Green Gang justify its use of violence and become legitimate to different political authorities in the early twentieth century?

Main Argument and Findings

The Green Gang did not rely on coercive power alone. It interpreted violence through culturally resonant claims about discipline, revolution, and nationalism. These claims, combined with practical participation in state building and resource extraction, made the organization useful and legitimate to successive political authorities.

Data and Methods

Historical-sociological analysis of primary and secondary sources on the Shanghai Green Gang, its public claims, its use of violence, and its relationships with political authorities in early twentieth-century China.

Contribution

The article bridges interpretations of gangs as modern criminal enterprises and as cultural descendants of secret societies. It demonstrates that organizational legitimacy can be built by reframing violence and linking it to state projects and public purposes.
